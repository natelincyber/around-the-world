Abstract

Abstract

Miniature quadrupeds under 250 grams have largely relied on hand-tuned, open-loop gaits. MiNI-Q's mechanically unbounded leg joints (continuous 360° rotation) open an action space that is well suited to locomotion discovered through reinforcement learning rather than scripted by hand. We present a complete sim-to-real pipeline — motor system identification, training in simulation, and cross-engine validation — that produces a single closed-loop policy running entirely on microcontroller-class compute on the robot.

The same checkpoint generalizes across body configurations and orientations, recovers after being dropped or thrown, and remains stable across unseen foot geometries and surfaces — all without retraining. This page collects the video, figures, and code accompanying the paper.

Approach

A single policy, trained in sim, deployed onboard

Sim-to-real pipeline

Step-response data from the real XL330 actuators is fit to a motor model, the policy is trained with PPO in simulation under domain randomization, and behavior is cross-validated across engines before deployment. Position-mode PD at the joints keeps transfer stable on a sub-kilogram robot.

Onboard, closed-loop control

The trained network runs as compiled C on the robot's onboard microcontroller, reading the IMU and joint feedback to produce position targets in real time. No external compute or tether is required.

Results

One checkpoint, many conditions

Upright and inverted

Under a gravity-conditioned reference, the same policy tracks velocity commands whether the robot is upright or flipped, with no retraining or separate evaluation.

Drop and recovery

Released from random heights and orientations, the policy returns to a stable trot within a few stride periods on the majority of trials.

Unseen feet and surfaces

The trained checkpoint sustains velocity tracking across foot geometries it never saw in training, over surfaces spanning a wide friction range.
